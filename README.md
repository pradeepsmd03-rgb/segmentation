# Customer Segmentation & Product Recommendation Model

**Internship milestone:** 4. Segmentation and Recommendation Model
**Tools:** Python, pandas, scikit-learn (K-Means, PCA, cosine similarity), Matplotlib, Seaborn

This project does two things for an e-commerce business:
1. **Segments customers** using RFM analysis (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) and K-Means clustering, so marketing can treat different customers differently.
2. **Recommends products** with collaborative filtering, and tests the models offline against a popularity baseline.

## Dataset
`data/transactions.csv` holds 32,091 order lines from 2,000 customers over 42 products in 6 categories (2024). `data/products.csv` is the product catalogue.

The data is **synthetic**, generated by `src/generate_data.py` with a fixed seed. Customers have hidden behaviour types and taste groups, so the models have real patterns to find. To use real data, keep the same columns. Because synthetic data has cleaner patterns than real data, expect lower absolute accuracy in production.

## Part 1: Customer segmentation

**Method**
1. Compute RFM per customer (snapshot date = day after the last order).
2. Log-transform frequency and monetary values (they are right-skewed) and standardise all three.
3. Run K-Means for k = 2..8 and compare elbow and silhouette scores. The best silhouette in the 3-6 range was **k = 4 (0.444)**.
4. Name each segment from its average RFM profile.

## Part 2: Recommendation model

**Models compared**
- **Popularity:** the most-bought products the customer hasn't tried (baseline)
- **Segment popularity:** the same, but within the customer's segment
- **User-based CF:** products bought by the 30 most similar customers
- **Item-based CF:** products similar (cosine similarity) to what the customer already bought

**Evaluation:** a temporal split. Models learn from orders before 1 Oct 2024 and are tested on Oct-Dec. The task is to predict *new* products a customer buys in the test period (741 customers qualified). This is stricter than predicting repeat purchases.

| Model | HitRate@5 | Recall@5 | NDCG@5 | HitRate@10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Popularity | 0.382 | 0.214 | 0.152 | 0.580 |
| Segment popularity | 0.385 | 0.218 | 0.158 | 0.583 |
| User-based CF | **0.753** | **0.549** | **0.434** | 0.862 |
| Item-based CF | 0.734 | 0.534 | 0.428 | 0.862 |

![Model comparison](outputs/figures/05_model_comparison.png)

The final model is **item-based CF**. It is nearly as accurate as user-based CF here, scales better as customers grow, and is easy to explain ("customers who bought X also bought Y"). It is refit on all data to produce `outputs/recommendations_all_customers.csv`. New customers with no history fall back to popularity (cold start).

**Example output**
> Customer 3 [Champions] often buys Cookbook, Planner, Bluetooth Speaker -> recommended: Webcam, Notebook Set, Data Science Book, Novel Bestseller, Phone Case

## Key insights
1. **Revenue is concentrated.** Champions are a third of customers but bring in about 68% of revenue, so losing a few of them hurts a lot.
2. **At Risk is the biggest opportunity.** 716 customers (25% of revenue) have a decent order history but haven't bought for about 5 months. Winning back even a fraction is worth more than chasing Lost customers.
3. **Collaborative filtering roughly doubles hit rate over popularity** (0.73-0.75 vs 0.38 at k=5).
4. **Segment alone doesn't predict what people buy.** Segment popularity barely beats global popularity, because category preferences are almost the same in every segment (see heatmap below). RFM tells us *how valuable and active* a customer is, while purchase history tells us *what they want*. The two work best together: segments decide the campaign and channel, and the recommender decides the products.
   ![Category heatmap](outputs/figures/06_segment_category_heatmap.png)
5. Strong product pairs found include Denim Jacket with Cotton T-Shirt and Leather Wallet, and Bluetooth Speaker with USB Cable. These are good candidates for bundles.

## Limitations and next steps
- Results come from synthetic data and a single time split. Validate on real data with several splits.
- RFM only. Adding category mix, discount sensitivity or channel could produce richer segments.
- Next steps: matrix factorisation (ALS / SVD), a hybrid model that blends CF with segment features, and an A/B test of recommendations.
